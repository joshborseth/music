# Agent notes

YouTube Music on the phone (Foldkit PWA) and the TV (Rust Android TV app), sharing one playback session. See `README.md` for what it does and how to run it.

The app is called **Joshify**, the sibling of Joshflix: the same monoline J, here with equalizer bars, in green (`#22d46f` on `#0b0d12`) where Joshflix is blue. The logo sources are in `branding/`; run `scripts/render-icons.sh` after changing them. Only what people see says Joshify. Internal names keep `music`: the Android package `app.music.tv`, the `music://` wake link, `music.service`, the `@music/*` packages, and the data paths. A new Android package would install as a second app that the installed one can't update.

## Finishing a task

When a task changes files in this repo, finish it by following the [`merge-and-deploy`](./.agents/skills/merge-and-deploy/SKILL.md) skill. It opens the PR, merges it, and deploys. Do this without being asked, unless Josh said not to push, open a PR, merge, or deploy.

Add new skills under `.agents/skills/<name>/`, and link each one from `.claude/skills/<name>` so every agent finds it.

## Layout

- `packages/shared`: the Effect RPC group (`MusicRpcs`) and the domain Schemas. Change the contract here first; both apps compile against it directly from source (no build step).
- `apps/server`: Bun + `@effect/platform-bun`. `src/youtubeMusic/mapping.ts` turns `youtubei.js` nodes into shared Schemas and holds all of the YouTube-specific parsing. `service.ts` is the `YouTubeMusic` Effect service. The app plays songs, not music videos: every list it reads from YouTube (radio, up next, mixes, playlists, moods, top songs) swaps each music video for its song (`songsNotVideos`), found by a song search (`audioTrackFor`), and a radio started from a video is started from its song instead, since YouTube's radio plays whatever kind its seed is. `rpcHandlers.ts` wires it to `MusicRpcs`.
- `apps/server`: `playlists/store.ts` is the `PlaylistStore` service: the playlists made in the app, Liked songs, and the albums saved and artists followed, and the play history (every song that played, on any device; the session hub records each play, and Home on the phone, through the `getHome` RPC and `src/home.ts`, and on the TV reads it), in SQLite through `bun:sqlite` (`PLAYLISTS_DB_PATH`). Each change is a `PlaylistEdit` (`packages/shared/src/domain.ts`) sent through the one `editPlaylists` RPC, which returns the whole library and the changed playlist. Add a table change as a new step in its `MIGRATIONS` list; never edit a shipped step.
- `apps/server`: `lyrics/` adds timed lyrics from LRCLIB to a song's details (`TimedLyrics.withTimedLines`, used by the `getSong` RPC and `/tv/lyrics`), cached in SQLite (`LYRICS_DB_PATH`). `lrc.ts` reads LRC; `lrclib.ts` picks the record whose length matches the song's. The phone (`apps/web/src/lyrics.ts`, `src/view/lyrics.ts`, the `FollowLyrics` Mount) and the TV (`apps/tv/src/lyrics.rs`) each turn the lines into rows from their own playback clock; keep the two in step.
- `apps/server`: `depth/` works out each cover's depth for the TV's Diorama scene: Depth Anything V2 Small (Apache-2.0; the larger ones aren't licensed for commercial use) runs through `onnxruntime-node`'s bundled CPU library in a one-shot, `nice`d subprocess (`runner.ts`, one cover at a time), and the maps are cached in SQLite (`DEPTH_DB_PATH`) and served at `/tv/depth`. The prefetcher warms the queue's next covers. The model downloads on first use, pinned and hash-checked, to `~/.local/share/music/models/` (`DEPTH_MODEL_PATH`; `DEPTH_MODEL_DOWNLOAD=false` turns that off). Without it the server runs as before and the TV leaves Diorama out (`can_draw` waits for the depth). Bun blocks the package's postinstall, which only fetches CUDA libraries; leave it blocked.
- `apps/server`: `montage/` makes each song's montage for the TV's Montage scene (`/tv/montage`, `/tv/montage.jpg`): it finds the song's official music video by a YouTube Music video search (`match.ts` turns down lyric, live, cover, and retuned uploads), reads it at 240p to 360p straight from yt-dlp into ffmpeg without keeping it (`extract.ts`, which stops early on a still picture), keeps the best frame of each shot and the 48 most varied (`frames.ts`), and packs them into one JPEG atlas (`atlas.ts`); a song without a video gets its artist's photos and covers (`artist.ts`). Cached on disk under a size cap (`~/.cache/music/montage`, 200 MB; `MONTAGE_CACHE_DIR`, `MONTAGE_CACHE_MAX_BYTES`); `prefetch.ts` makes them ahead for the queue's next songs, only while a TV is connected or waking (`tvWatching` in `session/hub.ts`, which gates the cover depths the same way). The TV cuts through it on the beat (`apps/tv/src/visuals/montage.rs`, `shaders/montage.glsl`; the lab's `montage.ts` and `dist/montage.html`).
- `apps/server`: `lanHttps.ts` serves the same router over HTTPS on `TLS_PORT` (4311) beside plain HTTP, both built once in `main.ts` so they share one session: the phone's Home Screen app uses `https://192.168.1.67:4311`, so it works on the Wi-Fi without Tailscale. The certificate comes from `scripts/lan-cert.sh` (README "Hosting"); the TV stays on plain HTTP.
- `apps/server`: also `audio.ts` (yt-dlp resolution and the `/audio/:videoId` route), `audioStore.ts` (the disk cache of whole songs, which every audio request goes through, and which streams a song while it downloads), `prefetch.ts` (gets songs ready before Play: the queue's next five, the page being browsed) and `session/` (`model.ts` holds the pure session state transitions, `hub.ts` the WebSocket, `wake.ts` the Home Assistant wake call).
- `apps/web`: a Foldkit app. Read `apps/web/FOLDKIT.md` before changing it; it is Foldkit's own convention guide. Playback lives in `src/playback/` (ManagedResources for the session socket and the audio element, their Subscriptions, Commands, update helpers, and `songCache.ts`, the phone's Cache Storage of songs) and `src/view/player.ts`.
- `apps/tv`: the Rust Android TV app, the only member of the root Cargo workspace. `audio/` is the playback engine, `controller.rs` reconciles session state with it, `ui/app.slint` is the screen.
- `apps/tv/src/visuals`: the now-playing visuals, GLSL ES 3.00 shaders (`shaders/`) drawn under the Slint UI with `glow`. Skia shares the GL context and is never told about our calls, so `gpu.rs` saves every piece of GL state it touches and restores it (`Saved`); keep new GL calls inside that. `music.rs` turns the server's song analysis (`TvAnalysis`) and the live FFT into uniforms: besides beats and sections, the server (`apps/server/src/analysis/`: `drums.ts`, `harmony.ts`, `moments.ts`, `score.ts`) tells apart kicks, snares, and hi-hats, the lead melody, chords and keys, stops, risers, and fills, and bakes the whole song into a score (a grayscale PNG) that the TV uploads around now as `uScore`, so scenes can draw music before it plays (`score` in `helpers.glsl`); `motion.rs` works out per-frame values a shader would otherwise compute at every pixel, `director.rs` picks scenes, and `sizing.rs` sizes each scene so frames stay on time. The server also plans each song as a VJ would cue it (`analysis/choreography.ts`: the climax, each build and the exact moment it lands, breaths, strobe bars, and how each section comes in), which `cues.rs` reads per frame, so the screen builds toward a landing by the seconds left (`uCueIn`) and hits on its frame. A change of scene freezes the old scene's last frame in its slot and only the new scene draws; `final.glsl` destroys the frozen frame (shatter, melt, zoom through, datamosh, wipe), each kind its own build of `final.glsl` (`TRANSITION`, `transition.rs`) so the picture between changes carries none of their code. `dist/moments.html` (`visuals-lab/moments.ts`) shows each change, a build into its drop, the glitch, and what a change costs. The TV's GPU is a Mali-G52: fragment shaders default to mediump (`fragment.glsl`), so anything that grows with time or becomes a texture coordinate goes in a `highp` variable (`common.glsl` explains). The same shader files run in the browser lab (`apps/tv/visuals-lab`, `bun apps/tv/visuals-lab/build.ts`); keep its passes, uniforms, `motion`, and `fbmPixels` in step with `gpu.rs` and `motion.rs`, and look at a change there (`dist/sheet-*.html` renders still frames, `dist/bench.html` ranks the scenes' cost) before shipping it. To see a real song, `cargo test -p music-tv visuals::lab -- --ignored` plays its samples and analysis through the TV's own listener and director (`visuals/lab.rs` says how), and `dist/song.html` draws the frames it writes (`labSong` in `lab.ts`): contact sheets of chosen moments and a timeline of the whole song. The passes are the scenes, the glow at an eighth of their size (`bright.glsl`, `blur.glsl`), `final.glsl` at their size (the tone, the vignette, and the app's shade), and `present.glsl`, the only pass at the screen's size, which reads one pixel and adds grain; keep work out of it, since the screen has two to five times a scene's pixels (folding `final` into it measured twice the shared cost in the lab). The TV posts its reports to `/tv-log?level=…` (`server_log.rs`); the server logs each line at the level it names, and a line without one, a crash, at ERROR (`tvLogLevel` in `apps/server/src/tvApp.ts`). Each change of scene is one of them, at INFO, at most one a second: the scene, how it changed, why (`director::Reason`), and the song's seconds, so a TV changing scenes too often can be read from this box. Don't use GL timer queries (`GL_EXT_disjoint_timer_query`): the TV's Mali driver advertises them, but with them nothing draws at all, not even the UI (#80). The lab can't show mediump precision loss. Anything in `app.slint` that fades out also sets `visible: self.opacity > 0`: below full opacity, Slint's Skia renderer draws an element with children into a screen-sized layer, even at opacity 0, every frame while the visuals run. Nobody can see the TV from this box: `POST /tv/capture` makes the TV send screenshots to `~/.cache/music/tv-frames/`.

## Conventions

- Effect 4 (`effect@4.1.x`). Versions shared across packages live in the root `catalog`. Keep `effect` on one version everywhere, because the RPC Schemas cross package boundaries.
- Foldkit and Effect APIs: check the vendored Foldkit source in `apps/web/repos/foldkit` (a git subtree pinned to the installed release) before writing from memory. After upgrading Foldkit, re-pin it with `git subtree pull --prefix=apps/web/repos/foldkit https://github.com/foldkit/foldkit.git foldkit@<version> --squash` and copy its `packages/create-foldkit-app/templates/base/FOLDKIT.md` over `apps/web/FOLDKIT.md`. `examples/api-cache-query` (Query), `examples/query-sync` (URL-owned search state), and `packages/typing-game` (Effect RPC) are the closest references.
- Remote data uses `Query.define` from `foldkit/experimental` (`apps/web/src/query.ts`). Each lookup is cached in the Model by its arguments.
- The search query and kind live in the URL (`/search?q=…&kind=Artists`); the Model holds only the text being typed. Typing waits `SEARCH_DEBOUNCE` and then replaces the URL.
- Native feel, in `apps/web`: each page scrolls in its own fixed box (`page` in `src/view/chrome.ts`, `.page-scroller` in `styles.css`), and the tab bar, nav bars, mini player, and sheets are fixed on top of it. The document itself never scrolls (`html` and `body` are `overflow: hidden`), as in the Lights app. Scroll code reads and moves the page box (`currentPage` in `src/command.ts`), never `window`, and scroll-driven CSS uses the `--page` timeline, not `scroll(root)`. Tapping the status bar does not scroll to the top, because iOS only does that for the document. The app uses no blur and no fades anywhere: no `backdrop-filter`, no blurred artwork, no gradient edges. Two exceptions, both made from the cover: the full player's background (`playerBackdrop` in `src/view/player.ts`), the cover blurred over the whole sheet and darkened toward the bottom on a solid color from the cover; and the glow behind the top of album, playlist, and song pages (`artworkGlow` in `src/view/collection.ts`), the cover blurred and faded into the page color. When a page's header scrolls away, a plain bar in the page color with a hairline covers the top (`.scroll-edge` in `styles.css`). Small floating controls (the tab bar, mini player, back button, notices) use the solid `floating` utility. In the Home Screen app (not Safari), iOS 26 fills the status bar with one solid color it reads from the page, and blurs the top of the page whenever it finds none. It reads the color from a `.status-bar-color` strip (`styles.css` explains WebKit's rule): one over every page in `index.html`, and one in the full player in the cover's color. Keep a strip the topmost thing at the top edge, never leave an invisible fixed layer above it (hide closed overlays with `visibility: hidden`), and start whatever sits at the very top of a page in the strip's color, as the cover glow and the full player do, or the status bar shows as a band above it. The page cannot draw under the status bar. `index.html` sets no `apple-mobile-web-app-status-bar-style`: with `black-translucent`, iOS never uses the color and the blur stays. iOS reads that meta when the app is added to the Home Screen, so changing it needs a reinstall. Page changes run through Foldkit's `viewTransition` (`src/viewTransition.ts`): a push slides in, going back slides out, and a tab switch cross-fades. `src/navigation.ts` records how each page was reached and where each page was scrolled; the `RestoreScroll` Mount puts a page back as it enters. Sheets stay in the DOM and slide with CSS (`.sheet[data-state]` in `styles.css`); the `DragToDismiss` Mount only adds a finger-following offset while dragging. Pages render through `memoizedPage` (`src/view/page.ts`), so playback ticks redraw only the player. Keep new page inputs in its argument list.

## Session protocol

The session protocol is defined twice: Effect Schemas in `packages/shared/src/session.ts` and serde types in `apps/tv/src/protocol.rs`. Change both together, and add or update a file in `fixtures/session/`. The server tests (`apps/server/src/session/model.test.ts`) and the Rust tests (`cargo test -p music-tv`) both decode those fixtures, so a field that changes on one side only fails a test. Keep wire fields plain JSON (`null`, never `Option`).

Volume is per device, not session state: `hub.ts` keeps each connection's `Volume` report in the device list and forwards `SetDeviceVolume` to that device as `SetVolume`; `model.ts` never sees either. The TV reads and sets Android's music volume in `apps/tv/src/volume/`.

Repeat and shuffle live on the server. Shuffle reorders the queue there, so players only follow it. What plays after a song is `Session.followingIndex` (and `skipIndex` for Next), mirrored as `following_index` in `protocol.rs`: the server, the phone acting on its own while locked, and the TV queueing its next song without a gap must all agree.

Autoplay lives on the server too. When two or fewer songs are left, `hub.ts` (`prepareQueueEnd`) asks YouTube Music for the last song's "up next" and `model.ts` (`appendAutoplay`) adds them with `autoplay: true`, unless the queue changed meanwhile. Players only show them: the phone under its own heading in the queue, the TV in the queue's summary line. Songs someone enqueues go before them, and `SetAutoplay` turns it on or off.

The server owns the session. Players follow `State` and report `Progress`, `Advanced`, `TrackEnded`, and `TrackFailed`. A player reloads when `currentEntryId` or `seekSeq` changes.

The phone is the exception, because a locked phone may hear from the server late or never. While it plays, it acts on its own controls and the lock screen first (next song, next, previous, queue taps, seeks, play, pause) and sends the change after (`SendLocalChange`). Each `State` carries `ack`, how many of that connection's messages it includes; the phone keeps its own changes over any `State` whose `ack` does not cover them yet (`LocalChanges` in `apps/web/src/playback/domain.ts`). On reconnect, `Hello.player` reports what the phone did offline, and the server's `model.ts` decides what to follow. Test both sides when changing any of this: `apps/web/src/playback/story.test.ts` and `apps/server/src/session/model.test.ts`.

## TV app

Build it with `scripts/build-tv-apk.sh` (see README). Slint's Android backend always uses Skia. The backend is a patched copy in `vendor/i-slint-backend-android-activity` (`[patch.crates-io]` in the root `Cargo.toml`): upstream drops the remote's media keys and its search and voice keys, and the copy sends them as private-use characters that `map_key` in `src/ui.rs` reads. When upgrading Slint, copy the new release's backend there and re-apply the `media_key` change. Voice search (`src/voice/`) is the app's one piece of Java: `java/VoiceSearch.java`, compiled to dex by `build.rs` and loaded from memory; keep its event numbers in step with `src/voice/android.rs`. 64-bit Skia comes prebuilt; 32-bit is compiled from source. `cargo test -p music-tv` runs on the host: there cpal is left out and a thread drains the audio ring in real time, so the engine runs unchanged.

Swarm and Coral simulate on the GPU before they draw (`visuals/sims.rs`). `particles.rs` is the engine: one particle a texel of an `RGBA32UI` texture, stepped by a full-texture pass, drawn as `GL_POINTS` whose vertex shader reads its texel, added on with blending; Coral keeps its reef the same way in `RG32UI`. Keep GPU state in integer textures (`floatBitsToUint`/`uintBitsToFloat`, read with `texelFetch`): ES 3.0 can draw into those, but float targets need `EXT_color_buffer_float`, an extension, so never use them. A scene's state binds to units 11 and 12 (`uState`, `uState2`), inside `Saved`. A simulation starts over on its scene's first frame (`Slot::fresh`), and one frozen while another scene takes over doesn't step; `particles.ts` and `coral.ts` in the lab make the same moves. Diorama draws its relief with a depth renderbuffer it attaches to its target only for its own pass (`draw_diorama`); `Saved` covers the depth state. It shatters itself only on a drop that lands while it shows alone, and starts whole when it comes in, since a drop that changes the scene already shatters the old one. Montage's `Cutter` (`montage.rs`) follows the cues too: it cuts on a build's landing frame and through the busiest pictures in strobe bars and the climax, and a cut never lights up while the screen flashes (a strobe bar, a landing or drop, the moment before one), so the two stay within three bright flashes a second (its test counts both). Its atlas goes up a strip a frame (`set_montage`), not in one 11 MB upload on the UI thread; it can draw once the last strip is up. A scene leaving keeps its frozen frame even when it can no longer draw (a new song drops Diorama's depth and Montage's atlas at once).

## foldcn components

`apps/web/src/components/ui` and `apps/web/src/lib` are copied in from the foldcn registry (https://foldcn.elianiva.com) with the shadcn CLI. Leave them as upstream ships them so `shadcn add` can update them; lint and format skip both folders. Because of these files, `apps/web/tsconfig.json` turns off `exactOptionalPropertyTypes`.

`shadcn add @foldcn/<name>` rewrites `apps/web/package.json` to older `foldkit`/`effect` versions. After adding a component, restore the pinned versions and the `catalog:` entries, then run `bun install`.
