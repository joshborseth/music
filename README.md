# Joshify

Browse YouTube Music on your phone and play it on the phone or on the TV. The phone app is a PWA built to feel like a native music app; the TV app is a native Android TV app written in Rust. Every device shares one queue, and the phone works as the TV's remote.

## What it does

- **Three tabs:** Home, Search, and Library, like a native app. Each tab remembers the page it was on. Tapping the open tab again goes back to its top. A page opened straight from a link sits under the tab that would find it: YouTube Music's songs, artists, albums, and playlists under Search, your own playlists under Library.
- **Home:** only your own music, never YouTube's general recommendations. First what played lately on any device, phone or TV, from the server's play history, each song once: an album more than one song played from shows under **Jump back in**, and the other songs under **Recently played**. Then **Your mixes**: up to four, one for each artist you play and like most, titled "Mix: <artist>". Each is YouTube Music's radio for their song you play and like most, and opens as a playlist with Play and Shuffle (its id is YouTube's own radio id, `RDAMVM` and the song's id). Then YouTube Music's radio for the last song ("More like"), **New from artists you follow** (their albums, singles, and EPs from this year and last, read from their artist pages, which the server keeps for an hour), your latest liked songs, your other playlists (Liked Songs shows once, as songs), and the artists you follow. A new phone shows the same Home as every other device.
- **Search** songs, artists, albums, or playlists. Results update as you type, and the last results stay on screen, with a spinner in the search box, until the new ones arrive. From the first letter, before any results, placeholder rows stand in for the moods and genres. **Top** comes first, as in Apple Music and Spotify: a **Top result** card (YouTube Music's best match: an artist, album, or playlist opens, a song plays, and once it plays the card shows it and pauses it), four songs, then shelves of artists, albums, and playlists. Each has **See All**, which picks that kind. When YouTube Music's best match is a song's music video, the card plays the song's audio track instead, found in a song search for the same words (`audioTrackFor` in `apps/server/src/youtubeMusic/mapping.ts`). When the best match is an artist, the four songs are the artist's most played, from their page, rather than whatever mentions their name. Song results put the songs by the artist searched for first ("daft punk", "get lucky daft punk"), then songs they are featured on, then the remixes and covers that only mention them (`rankSongsForQuery` in `mapping.ts`). When the best match is a song, its artist's songs come first, then their most played, and other artists' covers of it go last, so "blinding lights" lists The Weeknd rather than a metal cover. Remixes, covers, karaoke, and slowed, sped up, or 8D edits go last unless the search asks for one. Before you type, Search shows your last eight searches (kept on the device) and YouTube Music's moods and genres, each a tile in YouTube Music's color for it, unless that color is grey or another tile's: then it takes one from a palette, so no two tiles side by side look alike (`tileColors` in `apps/web/src/color.ts`). Each one opens a page of its songs, playlists, and albums.
- **Library:** your playlists, with Liked Songs first, then the albums you saved and the artists you follow, your downloads, then the albums, artists, and playlists opened on this phone (Recently viewed), newest first. A search field at the top narrows it to what matches, and chips narrow it to Playlists, Albums, Artists, or Downloaded; tap the chip again for everything. Under a chip, the order button sorts by Recently Added, Recently Played (when a song from it last played, on any device), A–Z, or, for albums, Artist (playlists also by Recently Updated, the default), and playlists and albums switch between a list and a grid of covers. Each kind keeps its own order and layout on the phone. **Clear** beside Recently viewed forgets those after you confirm it. Saved albums and followed artists live on the music server, so every phone and the TV see the same ones.
- **Tap a song to play it.** On an album, a playlist, or an artist's top songs, its list plays from there. A song in search results plays alone, and autoplay follows it with its radio, so the queue never fills with the covers and karaoke versions a search turns up. The ⋯ button opens a sheet to play it next, add it to the queue, start its radio, like it, add it to a playlist, or go to its album, artist, or lyrics.
- **Your playlists:** make a playlist in the Library, or from Add to a Playlist on any song. The ⋯ button on a playlist edits, renames, or deletes it, and ⋯ on one of its songs removes that song. **Edit Playlist** works as in Apple Music: the title and a description become fields, saved with **Done**; each song gets a − to take it out and a handle to drag it to a new place, as in the queue; **Add Songs** opens a search that adds songs one by one (a check shows each one added; tap it to take the song out again); and **Change Cover** picks the mosaic or one song's cover. An empty playlist offers Add Songs on its page. The TV shows the same order, title, cover, and description. The heart in the player and on every song adds it to Liked Songs, newest first. On an album, **+** at the top right saves the album to your Library (a check mark shows it is saved; tap it again to remove it), and **⋯** beside it, also on a YouTube Music playlist, plays all of its songs next or adds them to the queue (in one message to the server), starts its radio, downloads it, saves it, saves a copy of its songs to one of your playlists or a new one, goes to its artist, or shares its link (the share sheet, or a copied link where there is none). **Follow** on an artist's photo keeps the artist in your Library. Playlists live on the music server, so every phone sees the same ones.
- **Playlist page:** cover, title, who made it (linked when it is an artist), how many songs and how long, description, and every song (up to 500), with Play (filled in Joshify green) and Shuffle. Your own playlists show a mosaic of four of their album covers, and so does a playlist that only borrows one of its songs' covers, such as an artist's Top songs.
- **Song page:** artwork, artists, album, length, view count, lyrics, and a Play button.
- **Radio:** on an album or artist page, **Radio** beside Play and Shuffle plays YouTube Music's own radio for it: the "Start mix" on an album, the Radio button on an artist's photo, about fifty songs from many artists. Autoplay goes on after them. The button only shows when YouTube Music offers that radio.
- **Downloads:** the arrow at the top right of an album or playlist (yours or YouTube Music's) keeps its songs on the phone, so they play with no connection. The arrow fills as a ring while the songs arrive, one at a time, and the page says "Downloading 3 of 12", then "Downloaded · 48 MB". Tap the check to remove it. Library's **Downloaded** chip lists every download with how many songs and how much room it takes, the total, and the room the phone has left, with a button to remove each. Downloads stop, and say so, when the phone gets within 200 MB of what the browser lets the app keep. A guest on plain HTTP (no Cache Storage) sees no arrow.
- **Artist page:** photo with **Follow**, bio, top songs with their album and play count, albums, singles and EPs, and the artists their fans also like. **See All** on top songs opens YouTube Music's playlist of every song by the artist; on albums or singles it opens all of them in a grid, past the ten the artist page lists.
- **Album page:** cover, artists, release kind and year, total length, description, and the numbered tracklist with each track's length and an E on explicit tracks. A track names its artists only when they differ from the album's.
- **Playback:** play or shuffle an album, a playlist, or an artist's top songs, or add songs to the queue to play next or last. A mini player floats above the tabs. Tap it for the full player: artwork over a blurred backdrop, a seek bar, lyrics, the queue, and the devices you can play on. At the top it says what the queue plays from ("Playing from album" and its title, or a playlist, artist, mix, radio, or search), and so does the queue's Playing Next; tap it to open that page. The server keeps this with the session (`playingFrom`), so every device sees it. Pull it down to close it. In the queue, tap a song to play it, swipe it left to remove it, or drag it by the handle on its right to move it, as in Apple Music: the other songs slide out of its way, and the queue scrolls when the song nears its top or bottom. Songs autoplay added move only among themselves, after the songs you queued. **Clear** over Playing Next drops the songs you queued; the song playing and autoplay's songs stay.
- **Sleep timer:** the moon at the top right of the player stops the music after 5, 15, 30, or 45 minutes, an hour, or at the end of the song, wherever it plays: the server keeps the timer and pauses the session, the TV too. The moon then shows the time left ("14:57", or "End of song"); tap it to change the timer or turn it off. At the end of the song, the next song waits paused at its start. A phone that plays also stops by itself when the time comes, so a locked phone that lost the server still goes quiet.
- **Shuffle and repeat:** the buttons at the top of the player's queue, and on the TV's controls. Shuffle puts the songs still to come in a random order, and turning it off puts the list back in its order around the song playing. As in Apple Music, the big buttons set it: **Play** on an album, playlist, artist, or song plays in order and turns shuffle off, **Shuffle** turns it on and starts from a random song, and picking one song in a list keeps shuffle as it is. `PlayQueue` carries the choice (`shuffle`: true, false, or null to keep it), on the phone and the TV alike. Repeat steps from off to the whole queue to one song. Next still moves on with repeat one, and Previous from the first song goes to the last while repeat is on. A repeating queue gets no autoplay songs.
- **Autoplay:** when the queue gets down to its last two songs, the server adds ten similar songs after it: YouTube Music's radio for the last song, leaving out songs already in the queue. They keep coming the same way, each batch picked from the last song added, so the music drifts on from where you started. They show under **Autoplay** in the player's queue, after the songs you queued, and songs you add go in front of them. The ∞ button there turns autoplay off, which removes the autoplay songs still to come, or back on. It starts on. **Start radio** plays one song and lets autoplay add its radio at once; it turns autoplay back on if it was off, since a radio is those songs. A song tapped in search with autoplay off plays alone, as in Apple Music. The TV's queue says how many of its songs autoplay added.
- **Synced lyrics:** when a timed copy of a song's lyrics exists, the lyrics follow the song. The line being sung springs up bright, the lines glide up in a wave as it moves on, words light up one at a time when the lyrics are timed word by word, and a pause in the singing shows three breathing dots that light up as the next line nears. Tap a line to jump to it. Scroll to read ahead; the lyrics catch up a few seconds after you let go.
- **Play on the TV:** pick the TV in the player. If the TV is off, the server turns it on and opens the TV app. Playback moves over at the same position, and the phone stays in control. The phone can then lock or go offline; the TV keeps playing.
- **Where it plays:** the mini player and the player's device button always say where the music is: this phone, or "Playing on TV", "Paused on TV", "Turning on TV…", or "Reconnecting to TV…" while the TV has dropped off. When the device that played goes away and the music pauses, the phone takes over again, so Play plays on the phone. The speaker button at the top of Home opens **Play on** even when nothing plays. It lists every device with what it is doing: this phone, the TV (playing, paused, Joshify open, on with Joshify closed, or off, from Home Assistant), and other phones. Tap a device to move the music there. **Open** (or **Turn on** when the TV is off) starts Joshify on the TV without moving the music, so you can pick songs on the TV or watch what plays.
- **TV app:** works on its own with the remote, no phone needed. Four tabs along the top:
  - **Now playing:** the screen the app rests on. Visuals made from the music fill the screen in the cover's main color, made strong, the cover and song sit on the left, and every line of the lyrics runs down the right, the line being sung big and bright. A song without lyrics takes the middle instead. Over the cover, a line says what the queue plays from, as at the top of the phone's player: "PLAYING FROM ALBUM" and its title, or a playlist, artist, mix, radio, or search (the words searched for, in quotes), whichever device started it. Music started on the TV says so too: Play, Shuffle, or a song on an album, playlist, mix, or artist page, Play or Shuffle from a card's menu, and a song in search results each send it with the queue (`playingFrom`). A row of Home and a song played on its own from its menu play from nothing, as on the phone, and the line stays away. The controls stay hidden until you press a key: then buttons for previous, play or pause, next, like, the queue, and lyrics on or off come up along the bottom with the next song and the scan code, and the tab bar slides down. The scan code also shows for the first 20 seconds of each song, and is hidden the rest of the time so it never burns in. Up from the buttons selects the seek bar: Left and Right move the knob 10 seconds a press, holding one scrubs faster the longer it is held, and the song jumps there once the keys rest (OK jumps at once, Back drops it). They hide again after six seconds without a key (an open queue after thirty); Back hides them at once.
  - **Home:** the phone's Home, shelf for shelf, and only your own music: what played lately on any device (an album more than one song played from under **Jump back in**, the other songs under **Recently played**), **Your mixes** (OK opens one as a playlist to play or shuffle), **More like** the song played last, **New from artists you follow**, your playlists, your latest liked songs, the artists you follow, and the songs of your four most recently changed playlists, each under its name. Shelves with fewer than three cards go after the fuller ones, so Home starts full even before much has played. Home loads again on every visit and keeps the card you had selected; the server waits at most four seconds for the radio and the new releases, which YouTube Music fills, and a slower one shows on the next visit.
  - **Search:** an on-screen keyboard. Results appear as you type. Before you type, it lists your last eight searches whose results you used, kept on the TV: OK searches again, and holding OK removes one or all of them.
  - **Library:** Liked Songs and your playlists, then the albums saved and the artists followed on any device. A row with only a few cards says beside them how to fill it, and the last row says how to save albums and follow artists while the library has none of one or the other.

  OK on a song plays its row from there. OK on an album, playlist, or artist opens it, with Play, Shuffle, and Queue buttons, and on an album or artist a + button that saves the album to the library or follows the artist (a green ✓ once it has; OK again undoes it). **Hold OK** on anything for more: play next, add to the queue, like, save an album or follow an artist, or go to the artist. The queue slides in from the right, and says at the top whether shuffle and repeat are on: OK jumps to a song, and holding OK removes one. On Now playing and in the queue, holding OK also offers **Go to album** (or playlist, artist, mix, radio, or search) while the queue plays from one: it opens that page where the phone would, your own playlists under Library and the rest of YouTube Music under Search, and a search searches those words again. Up from the first song reaches **Clear**, which asks first, with Not now selected, and then takes out the songs queued after the one playing; autoplay's songs stay, as on the phone. While it is open, the lyrics make way and the song moves to the middle of the space left. Timed lyrics glide along with the song, the line being sung springs up bright, and pauses show breathing dots; plain lyrics scroll with the song. Back steps back, and pressing Back twice on the top level leaves the app. A minute without a key, with a song up, brings the app back to Now playing. The remote's media keys work from any screen: play and pause, next and previous, and fast-forward and rewind, which seek like the seek bar. Slint's Android backend drops those keys, so the app builds a patched copy of it (`vendor/i-slint-backend-android-activity`, see the root `Cargo.toml`).

- **Scan to play:** the TV shows a QR code for the server's address on the home Wi-Fi (`http://192.168.1.67:4310`). Anyone on the Wi-Fi can scan it and use the full app on their phone, without Tailscale.
- **Visuals:** five scenes of light in the cover's main color, pushed to full strength, from near black through the color to a pale, hot core: ribbons of silk drifting in a slow wind, luminous ink poured into moving water, a flight over mountains made of the spectrum, a ring of light that breathes with the bass, and a tunnel of light with a ring passing for every beat. The music picks them: calm songs and calm parts get the slow scenes, driving songs the fast ones, and a ballad's loudest chorus stays calm. A new scene takes over where the music changes: a new section, a drop (cut on the beat, with a flash in the cover's color), a chorus coming back to the scene it had before, or a phrase's end once a scene has shown a while: every 16 bars, or in calm music the first 16-bar phrase past 45 seconds, never in a section's last 8 bars and never back to the scene just before, so a chorus sung twice in a row can't hold one scene for half a song. While the TV isn't the one playing (paused, or playing on a phone), a slow made-up pulse keeps the calm scenes drifting. The screen shifts by a few pixels every 40 seconds so nothing burns in.

## How it fits together

```
apps/web         Foldkit SPA + foldcn components, installable as a PWA
apps/tv          Rust Android TV app: Slint UI, Symphonia (AAC), cpal (AAudio)
   │  Effect RPC over HTTP (/rpc, NDJSON)      catalog: search and pages (web only)
   │  JSON over HTTP (/tv/…)                   the same catalog shaped for the TV (TV only)
   │  WebSocket (/session, JSON)               the shared queue and who plays it
   │  HTTP with byte ranges (/audio/:videoId)  the songs themselves
apps/server      Bun + Effect server: youtubei.js for the catalog, yt-dlp for audio,
                 SQLite (bun:sqlite) for the playlists made in the app
packages/shared  The RPC contract, the session protocol, and every Schema
fixtures/session Example session messages that the TypeScript and Rust tests both decode
fixtures/tv      Example TV catalog responses, decoded the same way
```

**The session.** The server holds one queue for every device, and one device at a time plays it (the active device). Each device opens `/session`, says `Hello`, and then receives the whole session state after every change. Commands (play, pause, seek, skip, enqueue, transfer) go to the server, never directly to a player. The active device plays the current song and reports its position about once a second. A player that pauses says where it stopped, so every device agrees on the spot. A player that reconnects says in its `Hello` what it is playing, and the server follows it unless the session jumped meanwhile (a seek, a skip, or another device taking over). The sleep timer belongs to the session (`sleepTimer`): `SetSleepTimer` with minutes becomes a moment by the server's clock, which a fiber in `hub.ts` waits for before pausing (`sleepTimerRang`); with `EndOfSong`, players let the song end instead of going on without a gap (`stopsAfterSong`, `next_after_current` on the TV), and `TrackEnded` pauses on the next song. A timer survives a restart in `session.json`. Volume belongs to each device, not to the session: the TV reports its Android music volume (`Volume`) when it connects and whenever it changes, and the server passes a `SetDeviceVolume` request on to the TV (`SetVolume`). The phone doesn't change the TV's volume: its hardware buttons can't, because iOS gives web apps no way to read them, and the slider it once had was removed. Use the TV remote. The protocol is defined in `packages/shared/src/session.ts` and mirrored in `apps/tv/src/protocol.rs`. The server keeps the session in `~/.local/share/music/session.json` (`SESSION_STATE_PATH`), written a second after each change and again as it stops, so a restart or deploy keeps the queue and the song. After a restart the song waits paused where it was, and a player that kept playing through the restart takes it back when it reconnects. At start the server also tidies what was saved before the app played songs, not music videos: it cleans the queue's titles at once, then, in the background, looks up the song each music video in the queue and the play history is of and puts it in the video's place, with its square cover (`session/oldVideos.ts`). The song playing keeps its recording until it changes.

**Timed lyrics.** YouTube Music only gives signed-out clients plain lyrics, so timed ones come from [LRCLIB](https://lrclib.net), a free, open lyrics database that needs no account or key (`apps/server/src/lyrics/`). The server looks a song up by title, first artist, and length, and only trusts a copy whose length is within two seconds of the song's, so a music video with a longer intro never sings out of sync. It reads the LRC text into lines (and words, when timed word by word), turns lines nobody sings into pauses (credits such as "作词 : …" or "Lyrics by: …", and markers such as "Instrumental", "[Chorus]", or "♪", `isUnsung` in `lrc.ts`), and keeps every answer in a SQLite file so each song is looked up once; a song LRCLIB had nothing for is asked again after a week. A song page waits at most four seconds for a lookup, and the session looks up the current and next song ahead of time. When nothing matches, the player shows YouTube's plain lyrics as before. The phone and the TV work out the line being sung from their own playback clock (`apps/web/src/lyrics.ts`, `apps/tv/src/lyrics.rs`).

**The TV catalog.** The TV app browses without the phone through plain JSON routes under `/tv/`: home, library, search, albums, playlists, artists, lyrics, Liked Songs, and saved albums and followed artists. They reshape the same catalog and playlist store the web app reaches through RPC into shelves of cards and lists of queue tracks, and they get songs ready the same way when a page opens. The contract is `packages/shared/src/tv.ts`, mirrored in `apps/tv/src/catalog.rs`; both sides' tests decode the examples in `fixtures/tv/`. The routes are in `apps/server/src/tvCatalog/`. Home reads the same feed as the phone's (`apps/server/src/homeSources.ts` reads it for both, `home.ts` shapes it), so a mix on the TV is a playlist card with YouTube's radio id, which `/tv/collection` opens like any playlist.

**Playlists.** The playlists made in the app, Liked Songs, the albums saved and artists followed, and the play history are kept in one SQLite file on the server (`~/.local/share/music/playlists.sqlite`), opened with Bun's built-in `bun:sqlite`. Each entry keeps a copy of the song (title, artists, album, length, artwork), so a playlist opens without asking YouTube anything, and a song stays listed even if YouTube later drops it. A song appears once per playlist. The web app shows a change at once (a heart fills, a removed row leaves) and then takes the server's answer; if the server cannot save it, the app says so and reloads. A playlist plays through the same session commands as an album, so the TV app needs nothing new. `apps/server/src/playlists/store.ts` holds the tables and every change.

**Audio.** yt-dlp finds each song's AAC stream (format 140), using the proof-of-origin token service already on this machine (`yt-music-token`, `127.0.0.1:4416`). YouTube's links only work from the address that requested them, only with yt-dlp's User-Agent, and only a few seconds after they are issued. A request without a byte range is throttled to playback speed, so the server always asks for a range. The server downloads each song whole, once, into a disk cache (`~/.cache/music/audio`, 1 GB). `/audio/:videoId` serves a song with byte ranges while it is still downloading, then from disk. A song nobody prepared takes about 6 seconds to start: finding the link and YouTube's wait are most of it. A prepared song starts, and seeks, instantly for every device.

When the cache is full, songs played once or twice go first, least recently used first. Favorites (3 plays or more) go last, and the current song and the next five are never dropped. Play counts are kept in `plays.json` next to the songs.

**Getting songs ready early.** The prefetcher (`apps/server/src/prefetch.ts`) downloads some songs whole and only finds links for others. A song with a link already found starts in about a second.

| What happens               | Downloaded whole                | Link found only   |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Playing a queue            | The current song and the next 5 | The 5 after those |
| Opening an album or artist | The first 2 songs               | Songs 3 to 5      |
| Searching songs            | The top result                  | The next 2        |
| Opening a song page        | That song                       |                   |

Two workers take the most urgent job first, and a new page replaces the previous page's jobs. A song someone presses Play on never waits behind them: players go straight to the cache, and prefetching uses at most two of yt-dlp's three slots. To keep YouTube from flagging the server, prefetching may run yt-dlp 400 times a day, and it pauses for 15 minutes after 3 failures in a row.

**The phone's player.** The phone keeps up to 500 MB of songs in its own cache (Cache Storage), and asks the browser to keep it. A cached song plays from a local copy, so it starts and seeks with no network at all. A song that isn't cached streams right away and is saved for next time. While the phone is the player, it saves the current song and the next five, in queue order, again whenever the queue changes. When the cache is full, it drops songs the same way the server does; sizes and play counts live in localStorage. Downloaded albums and playlists pin their songs (`apps/web/src/downloads/`): those are never trimmed and do not count toward the 500 MB, and removing a download deletes the songs no other download holds. Seeking jumps within the loaded song, and play and pause respond before the server confirms. The full player has a seek bar that shows the position while you drag it, buttons to skip back and forward 15 seconds, and a spinner while audio loads.

**On the lock screen.** A locked phone may hear from the server late or not at all, and iOS stops a page that goes quiet in the background. So while the phone is the player, it does everything it can on its own first and tells the server after. When a song ends, the next one starts at once. Next, previous, play, pause, and the scrubber act on the phone right away; play and pause run inside the lock screen's own handler, where iOS counts them as a tap and lets audio start again. When the system pauses the song (a call, Siri, headphones unplugged), the session pauses too, so the play button works afterwards. Every state the server sends says how many of the phone's messages it includes, and the phone keeps its own changes until one does, so the answer to one press never undoes the next. If the connection dropped meanwhile, the phone reports what it played when it reconnects.

**The TV app.** Rust from top to bottom. The audio engine (`apps/tv/src/audio`) downloads each song into memory, decodes it with Symphonia while it arrives, and writes samples into a lock-free ring buffer that the AAudio callback drains. The next song is downloaded and opened while the current one plays, so it follows without a gap. The screen is a single Slint window drawn by Skia on the GPU, and it only redraws when something on it changes. Navigation lives in Rust (`nav.rs`): each remote key moves a plain state machine, which the tests drive, and `ui.rs` draws that state into Slint models, updating only the rows that changed. The now-playing visuals (`apps/tv/src/visuals`) are GLSL shaders drawn on the GPU under the Slint UI, through Slint's rendering notifier on the same OpenGL ES context, every frame while that screen shows. They follow the song two ways. The server analyzes each song once, before it plays (`/tv/analysis`: beats, bars, sections, drops, a loudness curve, and how driving the song is), so the visuals land on the beat, build up before a drop, cut on it, and give a chorus that comes back the scene it had before. The beat, the kick, and the hits come from the analysis alone: heard live, a bass line pumping between kicks fires a guess a quarter second early as often as on the beat. A frame shows a refresh or two after it is drawn, so the song's timeline is read 35 ms ahead of what is heard. Section energy is loudness within the song, so the song's drive (its hits and its grit, measured whatever its volume) caps how wild its loudest part looks: a piano ballad never reaches the tunnel. And `visualizer.rs` runs an FFT over what the TV is playing (`audio/scope.rs`, lock-free, read from what the audio device was handed so the window is centred on what will be heard as the frame shows) for the spectrum and the waveform. `music.rs` joins the two, `director.rs` picks scenes, and `gpu.rs` draws them: each scene into its own pair of textures (for trails), then the glow (the bright parts cut out at an eighth of the scenes' size and blurred there in one pass), then `final.glsl` into a picture the scenes' size, with the tone, the vignette, and the shade the app's words need, then `present.glsl` stretches it onto the screen with a fine grain. The screen's two million pixels are the most expensive in a frame, so the pass that draws them reads one pixel each and does little else; everything that can be is done at the scenes' size. Values that are the same at every pixel (the punch on the kick, the glow's strength, the flash, the vignette) are worked out once a frame (`motion.rs`, `Screen`). `motion.rs` works out once a frame what would otherwise be worked out at every pixel (where Ink pours, how Tunnel bends), and the noise the scenes read comes worked out ahead in a texture. The scenes are soft light and dark in one hue, with no picture to smear, because the TV's GPU draws them small and stretches them to the screen. Terrain's land is a mesh drawn over its sky, a row of points for each row of the spectrum's history, so each point reads its height once and the pixels read nothing. Ink and Halo keep their light as one amount in the picture's alpha and show it through the color, so trails fade through the color's own shades. The picture's tone curve works on its brightest channel, so bright orange never turns yellow. Every pass tells the GPU it covers its whole target, so the TV's tiled GPU never loads the old pixels back from memory. While one scene dissolves into the next, the two take turns, one a frame, so a change costs no more than one scene. Each scene has its own size (`sizing.rs`): it shrinks within a second or two when its frames come late, grows a step after ten clean seconds, and tries a size that already failed again only after minutes, so the TV settles instead of stuttering every few seconds. A scene that can't keep up even at its smallest moves at 30 frames a second while the screen keeps 60. Every five minutes the TV tells the server's log how smoothly frames came and each scene's size, and it reports any GL error once. The server logs these reports at INFO, and GL errors and crashes at ERROR: the TV names each line's level in `/tv-log?level=`, and a line without one is a crash. To browse, the TV reads the server's `/tv/...` routes (`packages/shared/src/tv.ts`, mirrored in `apps/tv/src/catalog.rs`), and it plays through the same session commands as the phone. The font is Inter (SIL Open Font License, `apps/tv/ui/fonts`). The app only speaks plain HTTP to the server, so it carries no TLS stack. The server relays album art at `/artwork?url=…`, for Google's image hosts only. If the app crashes, it posts the panic to `/tv-log`, and the report appears in the server's log (`journalctl --user -u music.service`).

**Waking the TV.** Home Assistant is already paired with the TV's Android TV Remote service. When you pick the TV while it is off, the server calls Home Assistant's `remote.turn_on` with `activity: music://play`. That turns the TV on and opens the app through its deep link. If the TV has not connected within 60 seconds, the phone shows a notice and playback pauses.

YouTube blocks cross-origin calls from browsers, so the browser never talks to YouTube directly. The server maps YouTube's loosely typed responses into the Schemas in `packages/shared`, and Effect RPC validates them on both ends. Artwork loads straight from Google's image CDN, and the service worker caches it.

## Run it

You need [Bun](https://bun.sh) 1.3 or later, and for audio [yt-dlp](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp) with the PO token plugin: `uv tool install 'yt-dlp[default]' --with bgutil-ytdlp-pot-provider`.

```sh
bun install
bun run dev
```

This starts the server on port 3001 and Vite on port 5173. Vite forwards `/rpc`, `/audio`, and `/session` to the server. Open http://localhost:5173, or the `Network` address Vite prints to try it on your phone over the LAN or Tailscale.

## TV app

The TV app needs Rust (via rustup, with the `aarch64-linux-android` and `armv7-linux-androideabi` targets), the Android SDK with build-tools 35 and NDK 27 in `~/Android/Sdk`, a JDK, `cargo-apk`, `ninja`, and `libclang-dev`.

```sh
scripts/build-tv-apk.sh          # dist/music-tv.apk for 64- and 32-bit ARM TVs
scripts/build-tv-apk.sh arm64    # 64-bit only, much faster to build the first time
```

The 32-bit build compiles Skia from source the first time, because rust-skia publishes no 32-bit Android binaries. The APK is signed with `~/.config/music/tv-release.keystore`. Keep that file: Android only installs an update over an app signed with the same key.

The app connects to `http://192.168.1.67:4310`. To point it somewhere else, set `MUSIC_SERVER_URL` when building.

To install it, open `http://192.168.1.67:4310/tv.apk` in the TV's Downloader app. The server serves the file at `TV_APK_PATH` there. Send Files to TV works too. Joshify then appears in the TV's app list.

After that first install, the app updates itself, but only when you ask. Each build gets an id (`<commit>-<UTC time>`), which the build script compiles into the app and writes to `dist/music-tv.build`; the server answers it at `/tv/version`. The app checks when it starts, every 15 minutes, and when you pick Check for updates in its Settings. When the server has another build, the app downloads it and the top bar says "Update ready". Nothing installs until you open Settings (the button at the right end of the tab bar) and pick **Install update**, so an update never closes the app or stops the music by itself. If a newer build arrived meanwhile, that one installs. The server then reopens Joshify through Home Assistant when it doesn't come back by itself within 20 seconds. Once, the first time, allow "Install unknown apps" for Joshify when the app asks. On Android 12 and later the update then installs without a prompt. Older Android versions, or a TV that wants a confirmation anyway, show Android's Install prompt instead. Failures show in the app and in `journalctl --user -u music.service`; pick Install update again to retry.

To see the screens without a TV, render them to PNG files. This needs a server with the `/tv` routes; it reads the catalog and artwork from it, and makes up its own queue instead of joining the session:

```sh
MUSIC_SERVER_URL=http://127.0.0.1:3001 cargo run -p music-tv --features screenshots --example screens -- /path/to/folder
```

The visuals' shaders also run in a browser. The lab draws the same shader files with WebGL 2, driven by a made-up song (or your own cover image), with a button per scene, or by a real song played through the TV's own code:

```sh
bun apps/tv/visuals-lab/build.ts   # then open apps/tv/visuals-lab/dist/index.html
```

For a real song, decode it, fetch its analysis, and let the TV's own listener and director play it, writing every frame of the stretches you name (`apps/tv/src/visuals/lab.rs` has the commands):

```sh
ffmpeg -i song.m4a -ac 1 -ar 44100 -f f32le song.f32
curl 'http://127.0.0.1:4310/tv/analysis?videoId=…' > song.json
VISUALS_SONG_PCM=song.f32 VISUALS_SONG_ANALYSIS=song.json VISUALS_SONG_OUT=frames.json \
  VISUALS_SONG_WINDOWS=14-18,60-64 cargo test -p music-tv visuals::lab -- --ignored
```

Then `dist/song.html` draws them: a script (Playwright) calls `labSong.load(frames, coverUrl)`, then `labSong.sheet({ window, picks, scenes })` for a contact sheet of chosen moments, each with the kick, beat, energy, intensity, tension, and drop that drove it, and `labSong.timeline()` for the whole song with the scene chosen for each part.

`dist/sheet.html` shows every scene still, side by side, and `dist/bench.html` times each one, the whole frame and the scene's own pass (`?screen=1920x1080&scene=896x504` for a 1080p TV holding a scene at 70%) (in a software renderer that only ranks them: the TV is far slower, and runs the shaders' mediump math at twice the rate). To see the real TV, ask it for screenshots while its visuals show: `curl -X POST 'http://127.0.0.1:4310/tv/capture?count=3'`. They land in `~/.cache/music/tv-frames/`. A frame that is all black, or all clear, is saved too, and logged as an error: "TV sent a black frame: the screen may be blank" (`tvFrame.ts`). The TV also reports its GPU, any shader that failed to compile, and every five minutes how smoothly frames come and how large it draws each scene to `/tv-log`, which shows in the server's log.

Tests run on this machine without a TV:

```sh
cargo test -p music-tv
# Plays real songs through a running server into a silent output.
MUSIC_TEST_SERVER=http://127.0.0.1:3001 cargo test -p music-tv -- --ignored
```

The logo lives in `branding/` as SVG. After changing it, run `scripts/render-icons.sh` to redraw the web app's icons, the TV launcher icon, and the logo the TV app shows.

What the TV app does not do yet: it has no TV banner image (cargo-apk can't declare one), so the launcher shows its icon instead. If you leave the app, Android can stop the music whenever it likes, because keeping music playing in the background needs a small Java service. The phone's own player has a short gap between songs.

## Hosting

The installed app runs on this machine as the `music.service` systemd user unit, from the live release in `~/.local/share/music/current` (see Deploying). The Bun server serves the release's built web app from `apps/web/dist`, along with `/rpc`, `/session`, and `/audio`, on port 4310. Text files go out compressed with Brotli (or gzip), each compressed once as the server starts and kept in memory. The hashed files under `/assets/` are cached for a year (`immutable`); `index.html`, the service worker, and everything else that keeps its name are checked every time (`no-cache`) and answered with 304 when unchanged (`apps/server/src/webApp.ts`). It listens on every interface (`HOST=0.0.0.0`) so the TV can reach it over the home network. The unit sets `YTDLP_PATH` and `TV_APK_PATH`. `AUDIO_CACHE_DIR` and `AUDIO_CACHE_MAX_BYTES` change where songs are kept and how much space they may use. `PLAYLISTS_DB_PATH` changes where the playlists are kept, and `LYRICS_DB_PATH` where timed lyrics are cached (`~/.local/share/music/lyrics.sqlite`). `LRCLIB_URL` points the lyrics lookup at a self-hosted LRCLIB. `bun run dev` uses the same playlist file by default, so set it to a scratch path when you try changes against real playlists. The unit also reads the Home Assistant settings from `~/.config/music/server.env`:

```sh
HA_TOKEN=<a long-lived access token from your Home Assistant profile>
TV_HA_ENTITY=remote.joshies_gamer_tv
```

Without those two values, the TV only shows up in the device list while its app is open. Tailscale Serve publishes it to the tailnet only (no Funnel) on port 7443 of this machine's Tailscale name: `https://<machine>.<tailnet>.ts.net:7443`. Run `tailscale serve status` to see the exact address.

On the iPhone, open that address in Safari, tap Share, then **Add to Home Screen**.

- Logs: `journalctl --user -u music.service -f`
- Stop publishing it: `sudo tailscale serve --https=7443 off`

The unit file is `scripts/systemd/music.service`, installed to `~/.config/systemd/user/` by each switch to a new release.

### Deploying

A deploy never restarts the server while music plays.

- `bun run deploy` (`scripts/deploy-main.sh`) fast-forwards this checkout to `origin/main` and builds it as a release: a copy of the repository at that commit in `~/.local/share/music/releases/<commit>`, with its own `node_modules`, web build, and TV app. It rebuilds the TV app first when `apps/tv` or the Cargo files changed (`--tv` rebuilds it anyway). Then it points `~/.local/share/music/pending` at the release.
- `music-release.timer` runs `scripts/activate-release.sh` every minute. Once nothing has played on any device for 10 minutes (the server answers `GET /session/quiet`), it points `~/.local/share/music/current` at the pending release, installs its `music.service`, and restarts the service. If the new server doesn't serve its own web app, it goes back to the release before. It keeps the live release and the one before it, and removes the rest. Several merges in a row go live together.
- `scripts/deploy-main.sh --now` (or `scripts/activate-release.sh --now` for one already staged) switches straight away, even mid-song.
- The phone loads the new web app the next time it opens. The TV downloads its new app after the switch and installs it when you pick Install update.

## Caveats

- `youtubei.js` uses YouTube's private API. A YouTube change can break it until the library is updated, and using it is against YouTube's terms. It is fine for personal use.
- Audio depends on yt-dlp and the token service keeping up with YouTube. When songs stop playing, update yt-dlp first: `uv tool upgrade yt-dlp`.
- yt-dlp solves YouTube's challenges with Bun. It warns that Bun 1.4 is newer than it has been tested with; installing Deno and setting `YTDLP_JS_RUNTIME=deno` is the supported route if that ever breaks.
- The server picks out some artist-page sections by their English titles ("Albums", "Singles & EPs", "Fans might also like"), so the session is pinned to English.
